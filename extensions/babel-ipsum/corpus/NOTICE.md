# Text sources and attribution

The bundled corpus combines public-domain literary works with transcriptions and editions from several sources. Attribution, edition descriptions, section coverage, and snapshot or revision references are recorded in each book's metadata under [`books/`](books/). Runtime **Information** shows the selected book's license note and links for selected and bundled sections. Those notes describe the source's stated terms; they are not a blanket claim that every modern edition or editorial contribution has been independently cleared.

## Reuse terms and attribution

For the Wikisource texts, the underlying older literary works are identified in the source metadata as public domain. Wikisource footers or page notices separately describe terms for contributor transcriptions, commonly Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0), sometimes alongside other notices or possible additional terms. Per-book metadata and retained source snapshots preserve the applicable source-page statements. This summary does not grant rights beyond those statements.

Project Gutenberg identifies the author text in _O Alienista_ (ebook 57001) and _Journey to the West_ (ebook 23962) as public domain in the United States, and supplies its [reuse terms](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html). Its [permission policy](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html) permits extracts and source acknowledgements and claims no copyright for formatting or spelling modernization. Babel Ipsum uses the name only in source acknowledgements, not as branding. These statements do not establish worldwide copyright status or identify an unrecorded print edition.

Aozora Bunko's [handling rules](https://www.aozora.gr.jp/guide/kijyunn.html) permit redistribution and adaptation of works whose copyrights have expired. Aozora catalog metadata is separately marked CC BY 4.0. The handling rules do not make every asset or item on Aozora's site freely reusable.

For _The Broken Wings_, Hindawi states that the 2012 edition's book text is public domain and legally available free of charge. Safahat's site footer reserves rights; that statement is not permission to reuse its assets, interface, or editorial material. The corpus contains the author's text and excludes publisher content.

For _Divina Commedia_, the nineteenth-century Buti/Giannini edition replaces the Petrocchi source. Scan-linked transcription metadata supplies CC BY-SA 3.0/GFDL notices; the site footer points to CC BY-SA 4.0. Those source statements are preserved in the book metadata. The prior 1994 date was not itself proof of active protection: Italy's special critical-edition right lasts 20 years, separately from any independently creative contribution. The replacement avoids having to resolve that edition's particular editorial rights.

Perseus identifies the Greek text as Storr's 1912 edition and licenses its repository contents under CC BY-SA 4.0 unless otherwise indicated. Publisher, edition and copyright evidence are retained separately from the extraction. Critical signs and bracketed source readings are preserved; no independent full scan collation is claimed.

The Korean 1925 original is public domain in the US from 1 January 2021, even under the full 95-year publication term ([17 USC §§304–305](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap3.html)). That calculation does not clear modern adaptations. Only eleven explicitly labelled original columns are included; 1925 scan fidelity has not been established. Source contributor terms remain CC BY-SA 4.0.

**Excluded sources:** [Godaan](books/godaan.json) and [Crime and Punishment](books/crime-and-punishment.json) are retained as development inputs only. They are absent from the bundled database and book settings because their edition provenance remains unresolved. Their metadata and acquisition records preserve the research for a future replacement.

Thirteen books are bundled. The explicit `BOOK_IDS` catalog in `scripts/build-corpus.mjs` controls inclusion; merely adding a prepared file does not bundle it. All fifteen prepared inputs, including the two excluded books, follow the `books/<book-id>.json` convention. Retrieval records, encodings, and snapshot hashes are tracked under `provenance/`; raw pages are cached under `.cache/corpus/sources/<book-id>/` outside the tracked corpus. Sub terra's original transcription, corrections, and scan evidence remain under `editorial/sub-terra/`; its editing details are in the [modernization policy](editorial/sub-terra/MODERNIZATION.md). That work is an edited placeholder-text corpus, not a facsimile or critical edition. Across the corpus, generated text includes selected literary passages only; attribution and source links are shown in **Information**. Ordinary Copy and Paste export plain text; **Copy with Attribution** appends the selected source and license information.

The [corpus licensing scope](LICENSE.md) distinguishes public-domain elements, applicable contributor licenses and project editorial adaptations. A copy ships in `assets/corpus-license.txt`; this notice and the prepared book metadata remain tracked for review.
